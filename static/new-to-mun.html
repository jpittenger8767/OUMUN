New to MUN?

What is Model UN?

Model United Nations is a simulation of the UN. Students take on the roles of delegates, usually representing countries, and debate real global issues in mock committees. Along the way, you practice research, public speaking, negotiation and writing.

How a conference works

Every conference is a little different, but most follow the same basic path.

1. Research your position

You're assigned a country (or another role) and a committee. Then you learn where your country stands on the topic, using the committee's background guide and UN sources.

2. Write a position paper

Many conferences ask for a short paper explaining your country's stance and the solutions it supports, written before the conference begins.

3. Debate and negotiate

In committee you give speeches, join moderated debates, and use unmoderated breaks to build alliances with other delegates.

4. Draft and vote

Groups of delegates write draft resolutions with proposed solutions, and the committee votes on them.

Key terms

You'll hear these a lot. Don't worry about memorizing them, because you'll pick them up quickly. Exact terms and rules vary a bit between conferences.

Delegate
A student representing a country, organization, or other role in a committee.
Committee
A simulated UN body, like the General Assembly or Security Council, that debates one set of topics.
Background guide
A document from the conference staff that introduces the committee's topic.
Position paper
A short paper that lays out your country's stance on the topic, written before the conference.
Chair (dais)
The students who run the committee, recognize speakers, and keep debate in order.
Placard
The card showing your country's name, raised to ask to speak or to vote.
Rules of procedure
The rules that decide how debate runs, who speaks, and how votes happen.
Speakers' list
The queue of delegates waiting to give formal speeches.
Moderated caucus
Structured debate with short, timed speeches on a narrower subtopic.
Unmoderated caucus
A break from formal debate when delegates move around to negotiate and write.
Working paper
An informal draft of ideas that may grow into a resolution.
Draft resolution
A formal document of proposed solutions that the committee votes on.
Sponsor and signatory
Sponsors write a draft resolution. Signatories support bringing it to a vote.
Bloc
A group of delegates with similar positions who work together.
Motion and point
A motion proposes changing what the committee is doing. A point is a question or request, like a point of order.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need experience?

No. Our meetings are always open to new members, and plenty of people start with no experience at all. Come to a meeting to learn more about the organization and meet other members.

When and where do you meet?

We meet every Monday from 7:00 PM to 8:00 PM in Farzaneh Hall, Room 148 on the OU campus.

What happens at a meeting?

Meetings are a chance to learn about the organization, meet other members, and hear about upcoming events. Throughout the year we also organize and host simulations, cultural events, and conferences.

Do I have to speak in front of a crowd?

Not right away. Many people start out listening and negotiating in small groups, then work up to giving speeches. Public speaking is one of the skills Model UN helps you build.

Do I need to study political science or international relations?

No. Model UN topics cover health, the environment, economics, technology and more, so students from any major can take part.
